# Sketches

One-page project sketches, written for a conversation rather than for a portal.

A sketch is what you send or bring when a professor says "what would you actually
want to work on?" It is not a proposal and it is not a research statement. It is one
page that gives a conversation an agenda.

## What a sketch contains

1. **The question**, in one sentence a non-specialist in the subfield could follow.
2. **Why it is open**: what is currently done, and where that falls short. Two or
   three sentences, with citations if you have them.
3. **What you would build or measure**: concrete enough that someone could disagree
   with it. "Evaluate X" is not concrete. "Compare X against Y on Z, measuring W" is.
4. **What you already have**: the evidence from `MASTER_CV.md` that makes you a
   plausible person to do this. Numbers verbatim.
5. **What you would need**: data, compute, access, a collaborator. Saying this
   plainly is a strength; a sketch that pretends to need nothing reads as naive.
6. **The first three months**: what would exist by then.

## Rules

- Every claim about your own work traces to `MASTER_CV.md` (CLAUDE.md hard rule 1).
- Every claim about the field carries a citation or is marked as your impression.
- One page. A sketch that runs to three pages is a proposal you have not finished
  thinking about.
- CLAUDE.md voice applies, and the em-dash hook covers this folder.
- Name the file after the idea, not after the person: a good sketch outlives the
  conversation it was written for and gets sent to the next person too.

# LinkedIn outreach log

**This log is not optional.** It is what the caps are counted from, and it is what
prevents approaching the same person twice.

The LinkedIn networking prong is **off unless `profile.md` enables it**. Read this
file before enabling it, so the decision is made with the numbers in front of you.

## What is permitted, when it is on

Exactly three write actions:

1. Company research (reading pages).
2. Connection requests to people whose role plausibly influences a hire.
3. **One** short introductory message to a person, after they accept.

Everything else stays barred at all times: InMail, messaging anyone who has not
accepted, a second message to someone who did not reply, following, endorsing,
recommending, commenting, posting, reacting, and any profile edit. If some other
action looks useful, ask first. Do not reason outward from the permission.

## Caps

- **5 connection requests per session.**
- **15 in any rolling 7 days.** Counted from this file, not from memory.
- **1 to 2 people per company, maximum**, ever.
- One message per person, ever. No follow-up if they do not reply.

These are deliberately conservative numbers chosen to protect the account. They are
not documented platform limits, and they are not a target to fill. The account-ban
risk is real and it is your account to risk, which is exactly why the caps and this
log are not negotiable by any skill.

**Changing the caps is your call, and it gets written down here with the date**, old
value and new, so the direction of travel stays legible. If you raise the session
cap, set the weekly cap in the same breath, or the first will breach the second
within a session or two. Whatever the numbers are, they are **the last constraint to
bind, not the first**: one to two people per company, and a real reason for
approaching each person, both bind earlier, and a run that runs out of good targets
stops there rather than filling the cap.

## Stop conditions

Stop immediately and hand back on any checkpoint, CAPTCHA, phone or email
verification, "unusual activity" notice, rate-limit message, restriction banner, or
session drop. Never work around one. Never retry the action that triggered it.

## Message approval phases

Counted by rows below with status `messaged` or later, cumulative across all runs.

- **Phase 1, under 12 messages**: every message is drafted and shown to you before
  it is sent, one at a time. This is calibration; your edits become instructions for
  every later message.
- **Phase 2, at 12 and above**: the template and the per-person variable line are
  approved once at the start of a run, then the rest of that run sends without
  stopping. You will be told the first time this switch happens.

You can send it back to Phase 1 at any time.

## How the messages are written

Calibration from a real Phase 1, shipped as the default because it held up. Your own
Phase 1 edits override it.

- **Short, and no evidence in the first message.** Three or four short sentences:
  thanks for connecting; one concrete, factual tie to their company where one exists;
  who you are, in one plain line; an open door, or a light question. No metrics, no
  method vocabulary, nothing whose purpose is to prove you are good enough. **The
  message earns a reply, and the substance goes in the reply.** A message that
  front-loads the evidence gives the other person nothing to respond to.
- **Write to the role.** A recruiter can be told you are looking. An engineer or an
  executive who is not a recruiter gets no job ask: close on a question about their
  work.
- **A connection note is the first message in the thread, not a separate artefact.**
  If the request went with a note, they have already read your self-introduction,
  and repeating it reads as a template: write the intro message against what is
  already there. If it went without a note, the thread is empty and a fuller
  introduction is right. LinkedIn opening the conversation as a "New message" is the
  reliable sign that the thread is empty.
- **Never assert something that happened at a different company.** "I sent you an
  open application today" is true for the company whose email went out and false for
  the one whose form is still staged. Check each message against that company's own
  state.

## Mechanics worth knowing

Observed 2026-08 to 2026-09. Interfaces change; re-date these when you re-confirm.

- **Free accounts get a small monthly quota of personalised notes** on connection
  requests, and the interface says when it is spent. Spread them one per company,
  toward the strongest hooks; the rest go as plain requests.
- **Connect is often inside the "More" or "..." menu**, on 3rd-degree profiles
  especially, where the visible buttons are only Message and Follow. A profile with
  no visible Connect button is not a profile that cannot be connected to. Follow
  stays barred.
- **Confirm every send on the page**: the button flips to Pending, or a toast says
  the invitation was sent. Creator-mode profiles never show Pending on the top card;
  confirm those on the Sent invitations page.
- Sending from a **search-result row** has closed the dialog and gone to the profile
  without sending. Send from the profile page, and check the button afterwards.
- **Acceptances are found by absence from the Sent invitations list**, not from the
  notifications feed. An acceptance a few weeks old leaves no notification that is
  still findable.
- Read a person's title from their **experience section**. Headlines are often vague,
  and a title search on the company page misses them.
- If a Connect control will not open its dialog after a few tries, leave that person.
- If the browser tooling drops mid-send, check the page state before retrying, so one
  invitation does not become two.
